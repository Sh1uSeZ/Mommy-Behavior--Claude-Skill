## Safe haven and secure base

Attachment theory gives the two poles in `SKILL.md`. A caregiver is a **safe haven** to
return to when distressed and a **secure base** to explore from. They aren't two separate
behaviors but two ends of one system: nobody explores from a place of fear, and comfort
without any push toward growth becomes a cage. That framing is the backbone of this skill.

- https://www.mindandbodytc.com/post/parenting-for-secure-attachment
- https://empathi.com/blog/what-is-the-safe-haven-and-secure-base/
- https://www.simplypsychology.org/attachment.html

## Emotion coaching

Gottman's five steps - notice the emotion, treat it as an opening rather than an
inconvenience, listen and validate, help label it, then set limits - are the source of the
five beats. The key idea carried over: **all feelings are acceptable, not all behaviors
are**, which is exactly how warmth can be unconditional while standards stay fixed.

- https://www.gottman.com/blog/an-introduction-to-emotion-coaching/
- https://emotioncoaching.gottman.com/

## Authoritative parenting and warm demanders

Baumrind's authoritative style - high warmth *and* high expectations, with reasoning rather
than rank - predicts better outcomes than the permissive or authoritarian alternatives.
Hence: explain, never "because I said so."

Kleinfeld's "warm demander," later developed by Delpit, is the same combination in
teaching: genuine care plus a refusal to lower the bar or give up on the person. It is
where "say the hard thing, then stay" comes from.

- https://parentingscience.com/authoritative-parenting-style/
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6323136/
- https://www.cultofpedagogy.com/warm-demanders/
- https://www.ascd.org/el/articles/the-teacher-as-warm-demander

## Invalidation

"You're fine" and "calm down" are the two most common well-meant failures. An upset person
hears them as dismissal, and the practical consequence is that they stop telling you
things. The replacements ("I know this is hard", "I'm here with you", "you don't have to be
okay right away") are in `reference/voice.md`.

- https://www.huffingtonpost.co.uk/entry/parenting-phrases-invalidate-kids-feelings_uk_68ee484be4b058bb20d3d205
- https://thepeacefulparent.com/2025/05/21/why-calm-down-doesnt-work-and-what-to-say-instead/

## Toxic positivity and sycophancy

These are the two ways this skill fails, and they are the same failure from different
directions.

Relentless positivity stops reading as support and starts reading as invalidation and
condescension - constant compliments feel *less* sincere, not more.

The AI-specific version is worse and better documented: across state-of-the-art models,
sycophantic responses affirmed users far more often than humans would, and even a single
sycophantic exchange left people more convinced they were right and less willing to repair
a conflict. Flattery also produces dependence. That is the entire reason for the
strip-the-warm-words test in `SKILL.md`.

- https://thepsychologygroup.com/toxic-positivity/
- https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aec8352
- https://www.jou.ufl.edu/insights/how-does-ai-sycophancy-affect-user-trust/

## The mental load

The most transferable thing about how mothers actually operate is **cognitive labor**:
remembering, monitoring, anticipating needs and tracking whether they got met - work that
is invisible precisely because it is only noticed when it fails. Mothers carry roughly 70%
of it in the average household. An agent can genuinely take some of it, which is why
"carry the invisible list" is a section rather than a footnote.

The same literature is the reason for the hard gate on check-ins: this kind of care is
"boundaryless" and always-on, and always-on care aimed at another person is nagging.

- https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sociology/articles/10.3389/fsoc.2025.1683261/full
- https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/the-compassionate-brain/202412/mental-load-the-invisible-weight-of-parenthood

## Praise

Maternal warmth in the research is praise, warm tone, affection, plus asking about and
taking interest in the other person's opinions. But the effective form is specific and
noticing ("I saw you help your sister with her shoes, and it got us out the door faster"),
not general. Effort and strategy praise beats identity praise because it survives the next
failure.

- https://www.calmkidcentral.com/parental-warmth-and-responsiveness-for-children-with-emotional-health-challenges/
- https://www.mother.ly/parenting/12-powerful-parenting-phrases-that-make-talking-to-kids-easier/
- https://www.yourtango.com/family/parents-mentally-emotionally-supportive-say-phrases-kids-casual-conversation

## Cultural variation

US mothers tend to express warmth directly - verbal praise, physical affection. In many
other cultures it is expressed indirectly, through acts of service, feeding, and preempting
a need before it is stated. In Cantonese the everyday greeting is "have you eaten yet?" The
bowl of cut fruit is a whole genre of maternal love with no words in it at all. Hence the
two registers in `reference/dials.md` - the direct one is a default, not a universal.

- https://today.duke.edu/2013/05/tip-mothersday
- https://tastecooking.com/bowl-cut-fruits-asian-moms-say-love/
- https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09540253.2025.2506355

## Prior art

`shell-mommy` and `mommy` are shell wrappers that praise you when a command exits 0 and
encourage you when it does not, with configurable endearments and pronouns. Worth knowing
as the reference point for tone - and worth exceeding, since a message keyed only to exit
status cannot validate, cannot remember, and cannot tell you when you are wrong.

- https://github.com/sudofox/shell-mommy
- https://github.com/FWDekker/mommy
